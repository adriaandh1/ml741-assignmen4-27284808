\section{Conclusion}

The self-organising map (SOM) produced a practically stabilised traffic representation with comparable training and held-out geometry, while log-standard preprocessing reduced contextual distance. Larger maps improved codebook fit but reduced occupancy and weakened local topology preservation, so geometric quality alone did not ensure reliable attack-class prediction.

The confirmatory classifier achieved macro $F_1$ of \ConfirmF, with full prevalence correction increasing rare-class recall at a severe false-positive cost. Post-hoc finer clustering with partial correction raised assessment macro $F_1$ to \ExploreBF\ at 400 clusters, accompanied by greater fragmentation and lower silhouette. Worms recognition remained weak, and the upper search boundary left the granularity optimum unresolved.

The $k$-nearest-neighbour (kNN) and classification-tree baselines retained higher overall macro $F_1$ on shared folds, while the exploratory estimate remained separate from the primary confirmatory result. Useful unsupervised geometry did not guarantee dependable class separation, while mapping exposed the precision--recall cost of prevalence correction.
