\section{Experimental Methodology}

The design separated unsupervised representation experiments from classifier selection and locked confirmatory assessment, with later sensitivity studies remaining post-hoc.

\subsection{Implementation and Training}

The SOM update and diagnostics were implemented directly with NumPy/SciPy, while scikit-learn supplied preprocessing and $K$-means clustering. Initial codebooks sampled training rows without replacement, allowing repeated vectors, while five fixed seeds measured initialisation variation. Validation supplied diagnostics without updating codebook vectors.

For map side length $\ell$, the 70-epoch schedule reduced radius exponentially from $\ell/2$ to 0.75 during the first 40 epochs, followed by 30 epochs at 0.75. The original schedule had reached minimum radius only at the final update, while error was still decreasing. Fixed-radius refinement was therefore locked before downstream label analysis and applied equally to every condition, without early stopping or validation-dependent updates.

\subsection{Representation and Downstream Development}

The representation experiment compared standard and log-standard preprocessing on $8\times8$, $16\times16$, $24\times24$ and $32\times32$ maps, producing 40 runs with five seeds per condition. Training and validation diagnostics were recorded separately, and no class-performance information informed representation decisions. The shortlist retained log-standard maps of sides eight, 16 and 32 to preserve different capacity trade-offs, while $24\times24$ maps remained geometric evidence only.

The original downstream grid combined the three shortlisted capacities with $K\in\{10,15,20,30,40\}$, comparing uniform and training-hit weights under majority mapping or full prevalence correction. Each codebook used five fixed clustering seeds, with $k$-means++ initialisation and 10 initialisations per fit. The Lloyd algorithm allowed at most 500 iterations with tolerance $10^{-4}$, giving 750 clustering fits and 1,500 validation evaluations. Training labels determined the mappings, while validation labels evaluated the resulting classifiers.

Class-score ties selected the lowest class identifier, and unsupported clusters copied the class of the nearest supported centre. Euclidean centre-distance ties selected the lowest cluster identifier, without chaining through unsupported clusters. An unseen observation passed through training-fitted transformation, BMU assignment, codebook-cluster lookup and mapped-class prediction, without neighbouring-unit voting or supervised correction.

After the grid, selection maximised macro $F_1$ over all 10 classes. Ties preferred higher balanced accuracy, followed by lower SOM variability and a fixed simplicity ordering. Validation therefore provided selection evidence rather than an unbiased estimate of generalisation.

\subsection{Confirmatory Assessment and Baselines}

The frozen procedure combined log-standard preprocessing and a $32\times32$ SOM with $K=40$, training-hit weighting and $\alpha=1$. Assessment used five geometry-safe outer folds from the reserved block, with preprocessing and five SOMs fitted on the other four folds. Outer-training observations also supplied clustering weights and class mappings, while development rows were excluded and held-out observations fitted no pipeline stage.

An out-of-fold (OOF) prediction comes from a model fitted without the corresponding held-out fold. Matching SOM and clustering seeds across folds produced 25 OOF vectors, each covering all 206,138 assessment observations exactly once. Metrics used the pooled predictions because averaging fold macro $F_1$ would give a different nonlinear summary.

Cover and Hart investigated nearest-neighbour classification~\cite{cover1967}. The frozen kNN used seven neighbours, Manhattan distance and inverse-distance voting, with votes restricted to zero-distance neighbours whenever present. Numerical features used training median/interquartile-range scaling, and categorical features used one-hot encoding. The classification tree, following the recursive approach developed by Breiman \emph{et al.}~\cite{breiman1984}, used balanced class weights, log-loss splits and maximum depth 16. Minimum split and leaf sizes were five and two, respectively, with pruning parameter $3\times10^{-7}$. Both Assignment 2 configurations were refitted on the same outer folds using method-specific training-only preprocessing.

\subsection{Post-Hoc Exploratory Sensitivity}

Sensitivity analysis was designed after the original assessment had been inspected, prompted by rising development macro $F_1$ at the cluster-count boundary and severe rare-class false positives under full correction. The later studies examined granularity and correction strength, but the assessment-informed design prevented independent confirmation.

Development exploration reused five frozen log-standard $32\times32$ codebooks and BMUs, beginning with $K\in\{40,50,60,80,100,150\}$ and $\alpha\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$. A final predeclared extension added $K\in\{200,250,300,400\}$, importing earlier results without refitting. The combined grid comprised 50 conditions, 250 clustering fits and 1,250 evaluations, with the original $K=40$, $\alpha=1$ anchor reproduced exactly.

Exploratory ranking used macro $F_1$, followed by balanced accuracy and lower SOM variability, with remaining ties preferring lower $K$ and $\alpha$. The first extension exceeded the predeclared 0.03 development-improvement threshold for recommending exploratory assessment, while the final extension retained a hard boundary of 400 clusters. Assessment candidates were frozen at $K=150$ and $K=400$, both using $\alpha=0.5$.

Exploratory assessment reused all 25 fold-specific SOMs and saved BMUs, refitting only clustering and training-label mapping through 250 clustering fits. Identical seed pairs produced complete OOF vectors, while confirmatory and baseline predictions were imported unchanged. No further model development followed exploratory assessment.

\subsection{Aggregation, Diagnostics and Uncertainty}

Aggregation averaged five clustering results within each SOM seed, then calculated the mean and sample standard deviation across the five SOM-level means. Mean within-SOM clustering standard deviation described remaining optimisation variation, without treating the 25 seed combinations as independent datasets. Structural diagnostics used labels only after unsupervised fitting and did not influence selection.

Fragmentation counts defined tiny clusters as containing fewer than 10 training observations and singleton clusters as containing exactly a single codebook unit. No cluster was excluded, and the counts remained descriptive without forming another selection score.

Efron and Tibshirani described bootstrap estimation through repeated resampling~\cite{efron1993}. The paired bootstrap resampled complete assessment geometry groups with replacement over 2,000 draws, retaining every observation within each sampled group. Identical group multiplicities applied to all methods, with macro $F_1$ differences averaged across clustering seeds within each SOM seed and then across SOM seeds. Percentile 95\% intervals described resampling sensitivity conditional on fixed models and observed groups, without $p$-values or claims of independent confirmation.
